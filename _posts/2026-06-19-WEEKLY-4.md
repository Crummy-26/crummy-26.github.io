## Research: 
- When studying with a past paper in unit 4 biology: Immunity, I came across the question:
	"One advantage of using hero rats to detect TB is that they do not get infected with M. tuberculosis. Suggest why rats do not get infected with M. tuberculosis"
- This led me to research more on the topic, and coming across a [paper](https://discovery.researcher.life/article/immune-response-to-mycobacterium-tuberculosis-in-rats/97985a78406a3e049d1faaa8ceffcc82) in NIH
### Summary
- Despite having injected directly with virulent and attenuated version of the same Mycobacterium Tuberculosis; the rats then showed rapid immunity against the pathogen
	- The marking scheme of the past paper highlighted two factors, being:
		  1. The **body temperature** of the rats are higher than that of humans'
		  2. The invaded pulmonary **macrophages** of rats were able to quickly **destroy** the pathogen before it could replicate
- The paper highlighted how the immune response was "immunologically specific" (Lefford et al., 1973) 
### Conclusive piece
- The rats have a much more heightened immune response to the bacterium in both virulent and attenuated forms than humans; with the specific immune response eradicating the pathogens so quickly that it looked as if the rats had a natural immunity against it; when in reality, the immunity was acquired.
### Links
- [Lefford, M. J., et al. “Immune Response to Mycobacterium Tuberculosis in Rats.” Infection and Immunity, vol. 8, no. 2, Aug. 1973, pp. 182–89](https://doi.org/10.1128/iai.8.2.182-189.1973)

### What did I learn?
- The higher the GC content, the greater the thermal stability
	- This is because Guanine-Cytosine base pairs have 3 hydrogen bonds, compared to Adenine-Thymine's 2 bonds per base pair
- There are two ways to import gene data from NCBI:
	- Use `Biopython`'s `Entrez` module
	- Use NCBI's official library `ncbi-datasets-pylib` and its `GenomeApi` module
- `Entrez` is like a messenger between Biopython and NCBI's database
	- The main command of it to use is `Entrez.efetch({db}, {id}, {rettype}, {retmode})`
		- where:
			- `db` = which NCBI database to search ("nucleotide", "protein", "gene", etc.)
			- `id` = the accession number or ID of what you want
			- `rettype` = what format you want back ("fasta", "gb" for GenBank, etc.)
			- `retmode` = how to get it ("text" gives you readable text, "xml" gives you structured data)
		- And it returns a "handle" object, like `with open(file.txt, "r") as file` in file handling
			- DO NOT CONFUSE the `handle` as a file. it doesn't contain the FASTA file itself. Its like the handle of a pan, you dont cook on the handle, you cook in the pan. The handle is used to control the pot - that's the distinction.
	Example structure:
	``` python
	handle = Entrez.efetch(db="...", id="...", rettype="...", retmode="...")
	data = handle.read()
	```

- For the accession number, there are different types:
	- `GCF_` = RefSeq genome assembly (the whole assembled genome)
	- `NC_` = RefSeq nucleotide (a specific sequence record)   <- I used this
- The output of `handle.read()` is one big string, which cannot be used to parse by `SeqIO`. So, use `StringIO` to pretend that string to be a parse-able file, enabling you to use it with `SeqIO`
	- `StringIO` comes from the `io` library, by the way.
